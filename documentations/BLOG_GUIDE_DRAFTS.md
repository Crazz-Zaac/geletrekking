## Everest Base Camp Trek Cost: Packages, Extras & a Realistic Budget

Suggested slug: `everest-base-camp-cost`

## How much does an Everest Base Camp trek cost?

Start with a real itinerary and an itemized quote. When this guide was prepared on October 18, 2026, Gele Trekking’s 15-day Sagarmatha (Everest) Base Camp itinerary listed an Economic package at **USD 2,099 per person** and a Comfort package at **USD 2,199 per person**, before any applicable offers. These are our package prices, rather than a claim about every operator in Nepal. Check the [current EBC itinerary and prices](/trek/sagarmatha-everest-base-camp-trek) before booking: your dates, room arrangements, and group size can change the final quote.

Your full holiday budget will be higher than the trek package if you still need international flights, equipment, insurance, or extra hotel nights. Compare the final trip total rather than choosing on the first advertised number.

## What does the package cover?

Our published EBC inclusions list a professional group leader and support crew, breakfast, lunch and dinner during the trek, and related trekking transport, airport transfers where necessary, and route permits. Ask for the exact transport sectors and hotel nights in your written confirmation. A phrase such as “transport included” should be translated into a list of actual journeys.

The Economic tier uses shared trekking accommodation and toilet facilities. The Comfort tier offers attached toilets where possible; higher lodges can have limited facilities. Comfort does not mean every night has the same room standard. Confirm whether the quoted room price assumes two people sharing, and request any single-room supplement separately.

| Budget item | What to confirm before paying |
| --- | --- |
| Trek package | Dates, number of days, group size, package tier, and currency |
| Domestic flights and transfers | Departure airport, both flight directions, and road transfers |
| Kathmandu accommodation | Included nights, room basis, and extra-night cost |
| Permits | Each required permit and whether it is included |
| Meals | Which days and meals are covered, including travel days |
| Support team | Guide and porter arrangements and luggage allowance |

## Extras that can change your total

The published package excludes international flights, optional activities, drinks, snacks and beverages, and personal insurance. In addition, ask specifically about showers, charging, Wi-Fi, equipment hire, tips, and any porter arrangements beyond those in your quote. Do not count an unlisted service as included or assume every lodge charges the same rate.

Make a separate list of equipment you already own. Buying a complete new kit is a different budget from hiring a sleeping bag and bringing well-used walking boots. Get hire prices and deposit terms before arrival, and check what happens if an item needs replacing during the trek.

## Keep a separate delay budget

A cancelled Lukla flight can change your hotel nights, meals, airport transfers, and onward ticket arrangements. Ask which extra costs you pay yourself and which are covered by your package. A helicopter alternative is a separate transport decision unless your booking explicitly includes it; never treat it as a guaranteed free replacement.

Read our [Lukla flight-delay planning guide](/blog/lukla-flight-delays) before choosing an international departure date. Preserve the agreed acclimatization days if travel is disrupted rather than making the walking itinerary shorter just to protect a flight booking.

## How to compare two EBC quotes fairly

Put both quotes next to the same day-by-day itinerary. Check the same number of Kathmandu nights, the same domestic airport, the same room basis, and the same meals. Then add the costs neither operator includes. A lower headline price can simply describe a smaller set of services.

Write your budget as **confirmed package + excluded services + personal spending + contingency**. Keep the contingency separate so you can see what is committed and what is still available for changes. Ask for a dated quote with payment stages and cancellation terms before transferring money.

### Are all permits included?

Our current itinerary lists route permits among the inclusions. Ask the team to name the permits for your route and nationality in your confirmation. [Nepal Tourism Board’s park information](https://ntb.gov.np/en/sagarmatha-national-park) is a primary reference; permit fees and requirements should be checked again for your travel dates.

### Is a private trip more expensive?

Ask for both a private and a scheduled-group quote for the same dates. Compare the total for your group, the per-person amount, room arrangements, and services. Avoid assuming that one option is always cheaper.

## Best Time for Everest Base Camp: Spring, Autumn & Month-by-Month Planning

Suggested slug: `best-time-everest-base-camp-trek`

## When is the best time to trek to Everest Base Camp?

For most first-time visitors, start by comparing **March–May** with **October–November**. Nepal Tourism Board lists these as the best-season windows for Sagarmatha National Park. That is a seasonal starting point, not a promise of clear views or uninterrupted flights on a particular departure. See the [official park overview](https://ntb.gov.np/en/sagarmatha-national-park).

The right choice also depends on your tolerance for cold, your available leave, and how much flexibility you have after the trek. Start with the complete [EBC itinerary](/trek/sagarmatha-everest-base-camp-trek), including arrival, walking days, and your return journey. Then look at the available-date calendar rather than picking a month and compressing the itinerary to fit it.

## Spring or autumn: how should you choose?

Spring is worth considering if you want to combine a high-mountain trek with time in Nepal’s lower valleys. Autumn suits travellers who put a high priority on post-monsoon mountain visibility. Nepal Tourism Board’s [climate guide](https://trade.ntb.gov.np/know-nepal/climate/) highlights spring rhododendrons and generally clearer skies in October and November. Neither description replaces the local forecast.

Compare the dates you can actually travel. A flexible departure that allows extra days at the end can be a better plan than a tightly timed trip in a supposedly perfect month. Ask what the planned sleeping places, lodge arrangements, and flight transfers look like for those specific dates.

## A month-by-month planning checklist

| Month | What to ask before choosing it |
| --- | --- |
| January | Is this departure running, and what cold-weather equipment and lodge arrangements are planned? |
| February | How does the itinerary account for late-winter conditions and changing transport plans? |
| March | Which dates fit your preferred pace and the full arrival-to-departure schedule? |
| April | Are the group places and room arrangements you want still available? |
| May | How flexible is the route and return travel if conditions change? |
| June | Is an Everest trip appropriate for your dates, or would another route suit you better? |
| July | What is the weather contingency, and do you have enough time for disruption? |
| August | Can your plans accommodate changes, or should you compare another season? |
| September | What do current local conditions suggest for your exact departure? |
| October | Can you reserve the preferred group and keep time after the trek? |
| November | What clothing and lodge details are recommended for the chosen dates? |
| December | Is the winter itinerary suitable for your experience and comfort requirements? |

These questions are a planning tool, not twelve separate weather forecasts. They help turn a broad seasonal preference into a route and booking conversation.

## What about winter and monsoon?

The park overview identifies December–February with snow and June–September as rainy months. If these are your only travel windows, ask for a current assessment of the route, operating lodges, and transport rather than assuming the standard itinerary will run unchanged. Choose your trip around the conditions and time available.

A quieter departure is not automatically an easier one. Ask what support remains available when the route is less busy and whether another trek would better match your goals. The most useful answer may be a different route or a different week, rather than a faster EBC schedule.

## Season choice does not remove flight uncertainty

Even a well-chosen month can contain disrupted flight days. Confirm whether your Lukla booking departs from Kathmandu or Ramechhap and how the road transfer is arranged. Keep your final international flight separate from the planned Lukla return and discuss a buffer with the team. Our [Lukla delays guide](/blog/lukla-flight-delays) explains the decisions to make before travel.

Check the full cost of the dates you prefer. Room arrangements, private departures, equipment hire, and extra hotel nights can matter as much as the season label. The [EBC cost guide](/blog/everest-base-camp-cost) gives you a checklist for comparing quotes.

### Does “best season” guarantee a mountain view?

No. Seasonal patterns describe typical conditions across a period. They do not guarantee a clear summit, a particular temperature, or a flight on a specific day. Build flexibility into the itinerary instead of relying on a single sunrise or connection.

### Should I wait for a last-minute forecast?

Use near-departure forecasts for final decisions with your guide, but arrange leave, insurance, equipment, and a realistic itinerary well before that. Ask the team about change terms before booking if your dates are especially inflexible.

## Lukla Flight Delays: Buffer Days, Ramechhap & Your Backup Plan

Suggested slug: `lukla-flight-delays`

## What happens if your Lukla flight is delayed?

Treat a Lukla flight as a weather-sensitive part of your itinerary. A delay can affect your departure airport, transfer, first walking day, lodge bookings, and return connection. Agree on the plan before leaving home so you do not have to make every decision in an airport queue.

Tara Air’s [published conditions](https://www.taraair.com/terms.jsp) describe weather and technical cancellations, rescheduling, and refunds. The conditions for your own airline, ticket, and operator determine your options. An airline refund does not automatically pay for every hotel night or transfer affected by the disruption.

## Confirm Kathmandu or Ramechhap first

Do not assume “Lukla flight” means departure from Kathmandu. Check the departure airport on the ticket and confirm it with your trekking team close to travel. Seasonal operating arrangements can change. Tara Air publishes operational information through its [reservation service](https://res.taraair.com/) and [flight-status page](https://taraair.com/flightStatus.jsp).

If you depart from Ramechhap, ask where and when you meet the driver, whether the road transfer is included, and where you stay if the flight does not operate. Keep that transfer separate from your international arrival plan: a flight ticket alone does not describe the whole journey to the trail.

## Build a buffer around the complete holiday

Our planning recommendation is to discuss at least two spare days after the planned trek return, with more flexibility if missing your international flight would be especially costly. This is a planning margin, not a guarantee that two days will cover every disruption. Tell the team if a work commitment, visa deadline, or onward journey cannot move.

Avoid placing your international departure on the same day as the scheduled Lukla return. Compare the cost of a flexible onward ticket with the consequences of missing a rigid one. Ask whether your travel insurance includes the relevant delay and missed-departure cover, and read its conditions rather than relying on a product name.

## What to do on a disrupted flight day

1. Stay in contact with the airline or trekking team and confirm whether the service is delayed or cancelled.
2. Ask what has actually been confirmed: another flight, a waiting list, or an alternative transfer are different things.
3. Keep the ticket reference, payment records, and any written cancellation notice for your records.
4. Confirm accommodation and transport costs before accepting changes.
5. Revisit the remaining itinerary with the guide, including the international return date.

Keep essential documents, personal medication, a warm layer, and a charged phone accessible rather than packing everything into checked baggage. Ask where you should wait and how updates will reach you if your phone has no data connection.

## Compare backup options carefully

A helicopter may be offered as an alternative, but it is not a weather-proof shortcut. Availability, operating conditions, landing plans, and cost must be confirmed at the time. Ask for a written price and exactly what it replaces in your original booking. Do not assume an airline ticket converts into a helicopter seat.

A road approach followed by additional walking can change the shape and length of the trip substantially. It needs a revised itinerary and current local advice. It is a separate route decision, rather than an interchangeable airport transfer. If your time is fixed, changing the destination can be more practical than forcing the original route into fewer days.

## Protect the walking itinerary

A flight delay should trigger a new planning conversation. Do not quietly remove agreed acclimatization days to keep the original finish date. Ask what can realistically be changed, what must stay, and whether postponing or selecting another trek is the better option for your circumstances.

The budget matters too. Extra lodging, food, road transfers, equipment hire extensions, and ticket changes can add up. Keep a separate contingency amount and agree who pays for each service before booking a replacement. Our [Everest Base Camp cost guide](/blog/everest-base-camp-cost) explains how to separate the package price from those extras.

### Will I automatically get a refund?

Ask your airline and booking agent to apply the terms for your specific ticket. Cancellation by an airline, cancellation by a passenger, and a change to a trekking package can have different terms. Keep the written confirmation of what has been refunded or rebooked.

### Can a guide guarantee a flight?

A team can organize bookings, transfers, and alternatives, but cannot guarantee that conditions will allow a flight. Be cautious about building your entire holiday around a guaranteed departure claim.

## Annapurna Base Camp vs Poon Hill: Which Trek Should You Choose?

Suggested slug: `annapurna-base-camp-vs-poon-hill`

## ABC or Poon Hill: the quick answer

Choose **Annapurna Base Camp (ABC)** if your priority is a longer journey into the Annapurna Sanctuary and you have time for its higher mountain stages. Choose **Ghorepani Poon Hill** if you want a shorter Annapurna trip focused on villages, forest walking, and a panoramic viewpoint. The better choice is the one that fits your available days and preferred pace.

Poon Hill is not a miniature visit to Annapurna Base Camp: the routes deliver different experiences. Likewise, reaching a base camp does not automatically make it the better holiday. Decide what you want to remember from the trip before comparing the number of kilometres or the final elevation.

## Compare the experience, not just the summit photo

| Your priority | Annapurna Base Camp | Ghorepani Poon Hill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main experience | A journey into the Annapurna Sanctuary | A shorter village-and-viewpoint trek |
| Time commitment | More walking stages and room for the higher approach | Often easier to fit around other Nepal travel |
| Walking style | Repeated days of ascending and descending before the sanctuary | Steep stair sections, forest trails, and an early viewpoint walk |
| Lodging questions | Ask about facilities at each higher overnight stop | Ask about village lodges and the sunrise-day schedule |
| Best match | Travellers who want a base-camp journey and have sufficient time | Travellers seeking a shorter Himalayan introduction |
| Cost comparison | Price the complete route, staff, transport, and lodge nights | Compare the same service standards and room basis |

Annapurna Base Camp is commonly identified at about 4,130 metres; the [Nepal Film Development Board’s location information](https://www.film.gov.np/destination/1) gives that elevation. Its higher stages are one reason to discuss the complete itinerary rather than treating the trek as simply a longer walk. Our [ABC itinerary](/trek/annapurna-base-camp-trek) shows the actual day-by-day trip we offer.

## How many days do you have?

Count from your arrival in Nepal to your international departure, then remove the days needed for transfers, preparation, and any other plans. What remains is your trekking window. A departure advertised with fewer days may begin closer to the trail than another itinerary, or may exclude time in Kathmandu and Pokhara.

As a planning exercise, ask the team to show you a Poon Hill route with three to five walking days and an ABC route with seven to ten walking days. These are comparison windows, not fixed packages or a promise that every traveller should use the shortest option. Our published ABC holiday includes additional travel and preparation days; compare full itineraries on the same basis.

## Which is easier for a first trek?

Poon Hill can be a sensible shorter option, but “short” does not mean flat. Ask about the stair sections, the longest walking day, and the descent. A sunrise viewpoint walk can start before dawn, so check what lighting, clothing, and support are arranged.

For ABC, ask where you sleep on each stage, how much ascending and descending is planned, and how the guide adapts the schedule. Tell the team about your recent walking experience and what pace you find comfortable. A discussion of an actual day is more useful than an “easy” or “moderate” label by itself.

## Scenery and season

Look through route photos and the day-by-day descriptions. If you are drawn to a broad mountain panorama with more time left for Pokhara or another part of Nepal, Poon Hill may fit your priorities. If the idea of progressing into the sanctuary is what excites you, ABC may justify the longer commitment.

Both decisions still depend on conditions during your dates. Use the [Nepal Tourism Board’s Annapurna overview](https://trade.ntb.gov.np/tourist-destination/annapurna-region/) for regional context and ask the team what is appropriate close to departure. A route name does not guarantee a clear sunrise, open trail, or a particular lodge facility.

## How should you compare costs?

Ask for two itemized quotes covering the same group size and similar accommodation standards. Compare transport to and from the start, guide and porter arrangements, permits, meals, lodge nights, and personal extras. A shorter route may reduce some costs, but different transfer or room choices can change the result.

Keep a separate allowance for equipment, drinks, tips, and any services excluded from the quote. Tell the operator whether the budget is per person or for the whole group. You can then change the route or comfort level deliberately instead of discovering the difference after booking.

### Can I combine Poon Hill and ABC?

Ask for a combined route rather than joining the shortest advertised versions together. The team needs to map the connection, overnight stops, and walking demands. Combining them makes sense only if it still leaves enough time for a comfortable itinerary and the rest of your holiday.
